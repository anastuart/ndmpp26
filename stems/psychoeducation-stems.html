Collaborative Psychoeducation

Stems for delivering psychoeducation the right way — by drawing on the client's existing experience first, introducing information briefly and collaboratively, and checking relevance at each step. The aim is to elicit insight, not transfer it.

Filter:
Opening — Exploring Current Experience
Before introducing any information, understand what the client already notices, knows, and experiences. This creates the foundation for everything that follows — and often, the client already has more insight than they realise.
01Tell me what [the behaviour / symptom / pattern] looks like for you on a typical day.
02Walk me through what [the time period / situation] usually looks like for you — start to finish.
03When you notice [the symptom] coming on, what's the first thing that happens — in your body, your thoughts, your behaviour?
04What do you find yourself doing in those moments?
05How long has this been a pattern for you?
06What have you already tried — and what's your sense of what helped and what didn't?
07What do you already know about [the topic] — what have you read, heard, or noticed yourself?

Impact — Daytime & Functional Consequences
Help the client connect the dots between the pattern and how it shows up in their life. Making this explicit — in their own words — builds the rationale for intervention without you having to state it for them.
01How does that affect you the next day — or throughout the day?
02What's the knock-on effect of that on [your mood / your energy / your relationships / your focus]?
03When this is at its worst, what becomes harder for you?
04How does it affect how you show up — at work, with the people around you, in yourself?
05What does it cost you — day to day?
06Has anyone around you noticed — and if so, what do they notice?
07Is there anything you've stopped doing, or do less of, because of this?

Comparing & Contrasting — Good Days vs. Hard Days
Drawing out the variation in the client's experience helps them identify what's driving the pattern. It also builds self-knowledge and often surfaces natural coping strategies the client hasn't named yet.
01What does it look like on the days when this is easier — what's different about those days?
02Think of a recent time when [the symptom / pattern] was less intense. What was going on?
03On the harder days, what tends to be different — what's there that isn't on the easier days?
04If you compare a good night to a hard one — what stands out as being different?
05What do you notice you do — or don't do — on the easier days?
06Is there a pattern to when it tends to be at its best or worst?
07What's something that has helped in the past, even a little?

Checking In — After Each Key Point
Rather than delivering a block of information and checking in at the end, pause after each key point. This keeps collaboration genuine and ensures what you're covering is actually landing — and relevant.
01Does that connect with anything you've noticed for yourself?
02How does that land for you — does it ring true, or does something feel off?
03What's your experience of that — is that something you recognise?
04What comes up for you as I say that?
05How does that fit with what you've noticed in yourself?
06Is there anything about that that doesn't feel like it applies to you?
07What's your reaction to that — does it feel surprising, or does it make sense?
08We've covered a bit there — what's standing out to you so far?

Linking — From Experience to Intervention
Make the connection between what the client has described and the intervention you're introducing — explicitly. The client should hear how the intervention follows naturally from their own account, not feel like it's being prescribed to them.
01Given what you've described, how do you think [the strategy] might be relevant for you?
02Based on what you've noticed, what do you think might happen if we tried [the approach]?
03You mentioned [what the client said] — that makes me think [the intervention] could be worth exploring. What's your reaction to that?
04What you've described fits really closely with what the research tells us about [the mechanism]. How does that sit with you?
05You've already noticed [what the client observed] — that's essentially what [the concept] is describing. Does that make sense?
06So if [pattern] is what's keeping this going, what do you think might help to interrupt it?
07How would you feel about us spending some time on [the approach] — given what you've just described?

Prioritising — What to Focus On Together
Once a few options are on the table, help the client identify what to focus on first. Use importance and confidence ratings (MI-style) to make prioritisation explicit and client-led.
01Of everything we've talked about, what feels most important to you to work on?
02On a scale of 0 to 10, how important does [the area] feel to you right now?
03And how confident do you feel that you could make a change there — again, 0 to 10?
04Why a [number] and not lower? What gives you that much confidence?
05If you had to pick one thing to focus on this week — what would feel most worth your energy?
06What feels like the most manageable place to start — even if it's small?
07Is there anything on the list that feels less relevant for you right now — something we could park for later?
08What would tell you that focusing on [the area] was worth it — what would you want to notice?
