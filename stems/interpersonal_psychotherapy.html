Question Stems

Interpersonal Psychotherapy

Question stems for the four IPT problem areas — Interpersonal Inventory, Role Dispute, Grief, and Role Transition. Designed to link interpersonal events to mood and open space for the client to explore what's changed in their relationships.

Filter:
Opening the Inventory
Wide, open-ended questions to map the client's relational world before narrowing to any one relationship or problem area.
01Can you tell me about the important people in your life right now — who do you feel closest to?
02What does a typical week look like for you in terms of contact with others?
03Who do you turn to when things are hard?
04How would you describe the relationships that matter most to you at the moment?
05Is there anyone in your life you'd like to have more contact with, or less?
06How has your social world changed recently — have any relationships shifted?

Mapping Key Relationships
Questions to build a detailed picture of each significant relationship — quality, frequency, history, and what it provides or costs the client.
01Tell me more about your relationship with [person] — what's that like day to day?
02How long have you known [person], and how has the relationship changed over time?
03How often do you see or speak with [person]?
04What do you appreciate most about your relationship with [person]?
05Are there things that cause friction or distance between you?
06What does [person] mean to you — how would you describe the role they play in your life?

Satisfaction & Expectations
Explore the gap between what the client wants from relationships and what they're getting — unmet expectations are often where distress lives.
01How satisfied are you with your current relationships overall?
02What would you want to be different in your relationship with [person]?
03What do you expect from [person] — and do you feel those expectations are met?
04When do you feel most supported by the people around you?
05Is there a relationship in your life that feels particularly unsatisfying or frustrating right now?
06How does [relationship] compare to how you'd hoped or imagined it would be?

Linking Relationships to Mood
The core IPT hypothesis — help the client begin to notice the connection between their interpersonal life and their emotional state.
01How do you notice your mood changing depending on how things are going with the people around you?
02When things are tense with [person], what does that do to how you're feeling overall?
03Are there times when feeling connected to others lifts your mood? What does that look like?
04When you're feeling low, what tends to happen in your relationships?
05How do you think the people around you have noticed the changes you've been going through?
06What comes first for you — feeling low, or things becoming harder in your relationships?

Opening the Dispute
Invite the client to describe the conflict in their own words before forming any clinical picture of the stage or pattern involved.
01It sounds like things have been tense with [person] — can you walk me through what that usually looks like?
02When did you first notice things starting to feel different between you?
03How would you describe the conflict in your own words?
04What usually triggers things to flare up between you?
05When things are at their worst with [person], what does that look like?
06How long has this been going on?
Role DisputeOpeningAssessment
Stage of the Dispute
Assess whether the dispute is in active negotiation, impasse, or dissolution — the stage shapes the therapeutic focus.
01Where do you feel things are at right now — is this something you're still trying to work through, or does it feel more stuck?
02How much communication is happening between you at the moment?
03Does [person] know how you're feeling about all of this?
04Do you feel like the relationship still has something worth fighting for?
05Have you and [person] talked about what each of you wants from this relationship?
06At what point would you feel like it was no longer worth trying to resolve this?

Communication Patterns
Examine how the client expresses themselves (or doesn't) in the conflict — poor communication maintenance is often the proximal target in IPT dispute work.
01When you try to raise this with [person], how does it usually go?
02How do you tend to respond when [person] does [behaviour]?
03Do you find it easy or hard to let [person] know how their actions affect you?
04Have you been able to let [person] know how their involvement affects you?
05What happens when you hold back from saying what you're thinking?
06How do you think [person] would describe their side of this?
07What gets in the way of the two of you being able to talk this through?

What Needs to Change
Help the client identify what they need from the relationship — and what they might be willing to offer — before generating options for change.
01What do you wish could be different in that relationship?
02What would need to change for things to feel better between you?
03What do you need from [person] that you're not getting right now?
04What do you think [person] needs from you?
05Is there something you could do differently that might shift how things go?
06If the relationship were in a better place, what would it look like?

Resolving or Moving Forward
Whether the goal is resolution, renegotiation, or acceptance — help the client think through what moving forward actually means for them.
01How have you managed disagreements with [person] in the past?
02What's worked before when things have been hard between you?
03Is there a way to have a different kind of conversation with [person] — one that feels less likely to end in conflict?
04What would it take to feel at peace with how things are, even if they don't change completely?
05Are there parts of the relationship worth preserving even if other parts are difficult?
06What does moving forward look like for you — is it about resolving this, accepting it, or something else?

Opening the Loss
Gentle, permission-seeking opening to the grief domain — name the loss without rushing to explore it before the client is ready.
01I noticed you mentioned losing [person] — would it be okay to talk a little about that?
02How long ago did that happen?
03Can you tell me a bit about [person] — who they were to you?
04What were the circumstances of the loss?
05Who was with you during that time?
06How did you first find out, or first experience the loss?

Exploring the Grief
Invite the client to describe what the grief has been like — its texture, rhythm, and what they've missed most.
01How has that loss stayed with you over the years / months?
02Are there times when the grief feels more present than others?
03What has grieving looked like for you — how have you carried it?
04What do you miss most about [person]?
05Have you been able to talk about this with anyone?
06How have things changed since [person] died?
GriefBereavementExploration
Complicated or Prolonged Grief
Explore signs of complicated grief — avoidance, unfinished business, blocked mourning — without pathologising the client's experience.
01Do you ever feel like you haven't been able to fully grieve?
02Have there been times when you've pushed the grief aside — what made you do that?
03Are there unfinished things between you and [person] that still feel present?
04What has it been like to carry that loss while also managing everything else?
05Have you noticed the grief interfering with your daily life or relationships?
06What would it mean to let yourself grieve more fully?

Linking Loss to Current Functioning
Help the client draw the thread between their grief and their current mood, relationships, and daily life — establishing the IPT focus.
01How do you think the loss has shaped who you are now, or how you relate to people?
02Do you notice the loss affecting how you approach new relationships or experiences?
03Are there things you've stopped doing, or started doing, because of this loss?
04How has the grief changed your relationships with people who knew [person]?
05In what ways has the loss made things harder for you recently?
06How does the loss connect to how you've been feeling lately?

Opening the Transition
Invite the client to name and describe the change — what it has involved, when it started, and how they've experienced it so far.
01You've been going through a significant change — can you tell me what that's been like for you?
02What was it like when things first shifted?
03How did you imagine this transition would be — and how has it compared to that?
04When did you first notice the change really affecting you?
05What do you find yourself thinking about most when it comes to this change?
06How do you feel about where you are now, in relation to where you were?
Role TransitionOpeningAssessment
Loss of the Old Role
Help the client grieve what the old role provided — identity, structure, purpose, connection — before building the new one.
01What did [old role] mean to you — what did it give you beyond the practical?
02What do you miss most about your life when [old role] was part of it?
03How did [old role] shape how you saw yourself?
04Who were you in relation to others when [old role] was intact?
05What parts of yourself do you feel like you're grieving as part of this change?
06What did [old role] give you that feels hard to find now?

Exploring the New Role
Explore what the new role is actually like to live — its challenges, its unfamiliarity, and any emerging possibilities.
01How would you describe your life now, day to day?
02What does a typical day look like for you at the moment?
03What parts of this new chapter have been the hardest to adjust to?
04What feels most unfamiliar or uncomfortable about where you are now?
05What does [new situation] mean to you now that you're living it?
06Has anything surprised you — positively or negatively — about this change?

Linking Transition to Mood & Relationships
Draw out the connection between the role change and both the client's emotional state and their interpersonal world.
01When you think about not having [old role] anymore, what comes up for you emotionally?
02How do you think the shift has affected your relationships with others?
03Do you notice your mood being connected to how purposeful or connected you feel on a given day?
04How have the people around you responded to this change?
05Has the transition affected any relationships in ways you didn't expect?
06What do you need from others right now that you might not be getting?

Building the New Role
Help the client move toward the new role with intention — identifying strengths to carry forward and possibilities to move toward.
01Are there things you've wanted to explore or try that you hadn't had time or space for before?
02What would a meaningful day look like for you now?
03What would need to change for you to feel more settled in this stage of your life?
04Are there parts of who you were in [old role] that you could carry into something new?
05What strengths from your old role might serve you in this new chapter?
06What would feeling at home in this new phase of your life look like?
07What's one small step that might help you feel more grounded or purposeful right now?
