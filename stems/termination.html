Guide for Termination

Stems for the ending phase of therapy — preparing clients from the start, gauging readiness, reviewing gains, building a maintenance plan, and saying goodbye well. The aim is to leave the client confident they can carry the work forward without you.

Groundwork — Setting Up the End From the Start
Termination is shaped from session one. Frame therapy as time-limited, position the client as the agent of change, and make ending a positive goal rather than a loss. Revisit the timeline often so it never lands as a surprise.
01Therapy is meant to be time-limited — the goal is for you to leave with skills you can use without me. How does that sit with you?
02Under [the Mental Health Care Plan / your funding] we have around [10] sessions to work with. How would you like to use that time?
03What would tell you, by the end of our work together, that therapy had been worth doing?
04If we imagine the version of you who no longer needs to be here — what's different about them?
05My job is to work myself out of a job — what do you think that looks like for you?
06We're going to be checking in regularly on how things are tracking towards ending — does that feel okay to you?
07What's your understanding of what we're aiming for here, in your own words?

Goals & Treatment Plan — Anchoring the Work
Clear, specific, measurable goals give every session direction and make termination concrete rather than ambiguous. Refer back to the plan often, revise when it stops fitting, and let goal attainment be what cues the conversation about ending.
01If we're going to know when we're done, our goals need to be specific enough to measure — how would you know [the goal] had been met?
02Looking at the treatment plan we set up — where do you feel you've moved, and where is there still work to do?
03Has anything changed since we set these goals — do they still feel like the right targets for you?
04If you had to put a percentage on how close you feel to [the goal] — what would you say?
05What would the last five percent look like — what's still in the way?
06Is there a goal here that no longer feels relevant — something we could let go of?
07What's a sign you'd want to see, week to week, that would tell you we're on track?

Gauging Readiness — Is It Time to End?
Readiness isn't only symptom reduction — it's also functioning, confidence in coping, and the clinical judgement that further therapy is unlikely to add more. Check from several angles, and be willing to name when ending is right even if the client is hesitant.
01When you compare where you are now to where you were when we started — what's shifted?
02How are things going at [home / work / school / in your relationships] compared to a few months ago?
03If you imagine us stopping in [two / three] sessions — what comes up for you?
04How confident do you feel — out of ten — in handling things on your own from here?
05What still feels like it would benefit from us continuing to work on it together?
06Is there anything you've been holding back from bringing in because we're getting near the end?
07What's your honest sense — are we still moving, or have we plateaued?
08My sense is that we've come a long way — and that the work that's left might be something you can carry from here. How does that land?

Reviewing Gains — Consolidating What's Changed
Make the changes explicit, in the client's own words. This consolidates learning, builds self-efficacy, and gives the client something to take with them. Pre/post measures (DASS-21, K10, BDI/BAI etc.) can complement the conversation.
01Looking back over our work, what feels most meaningful or valuable to you?
02What have you learnt about yourself in this process?
03What positive changes have you noticed in your life — at home, at work, in your relationships, in yourself?
04What's something you can do now that you couldn't do — or didn't believe you could do — at the start?
05If you compared your [DASS / K10 / BDI] scores from the beginning to now, what stands out?
06Have other people in your life noticed any changes — what have they reflected back to you?
07Of all the things we've worked on, which tools or ideas have stuck — the ones you actually use?
08How will you continue to use what you've learned once we stop meeting?
09If you had to sum up what therapy has given you in a sentence — what would you say?

Maintenance & Relapse Prevention — Planning for What's Next
A maintenance plan turns the work into something portable. Help the client identify their triggers and warning signs, name what they'll do early, and decide in advance what would tell them to seek help again.
01What kinds of situations, people, or stressors tend to bring this back for you?
02If things were starting to slide, what would be the first warning signs — in your thoughts, your body, your behaviour?
03What would future-you want present-you to do at the first hint of [the warning sign]?
04Of everything we've worked on, what are the two or three tools you most want to keep using?
05What would tell you it's time to come back to therapy — what's your line in the sand?
06A setback isn't a relapse — what would help you treat a hard week as information rather than failure?
07Who in your life knows enough to support you if you noticed warning signs returning?
08What self-care or routine matters most to keep in place — the non-negotiables?
09If we were writing this plan as a letter to yourself in six months, what would you want it to say?

Saying Goodbye — Closing the Relationship Well
The final session is its own piece of work. Acknowledge the relationship, make space for ambivalence about ending, and be clear about what's possible if the client needs to come back.
01How are you feeling about this being our last session?
02Endings can bring up a mix of things — relief, sadness, gratitude, uncertainty. What's there for you?
03Is there anything you've wanted to say but haven't yet?
04Is there anything you'd like to ask me before we finish?
05If something comes up down the track, what's your plan for getting in touch — with me, your GP, or another support?
06What do you want to take with you from our work — a phrase, an image, a tool?
07If you imagine looking back on this work in a year's time, what would you hope you'd be able to say?
08Thank you for the work you've done here — is there anything else you want to mark before we close?
